# Independent holistic review: clay-weathering-steel-footbridge v001

Reviewer: independent RLASM v6.1 holistic reviewer (not the builder). Evidence used: only the rendered pixels in `renders/*.png` (all 18 opened at full resolution), the phone boards in `boards/*.png`, and the locked sources `sources/front.png`, `sources/oblique.jpg` and `sources/top.jpg`. Builder metadata, counts and notes were not used as proof. Zoomed crops for this review are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-CityPrompt/18c9e177-8a96-5987-8fce-7444c75d018c/scratchpad/review-crops-corten-8ddb2199/`.

## 1. Verdict

**VISUAL_REWORK_REQUIRED.** The asset clearly reads as a rust-orange Warren-truss footbridge with a V-shaped plan over a city road. It still has six P1 defects. The apex is an invented hexagonal pavilion where the source has a sharp mitred corner, the V is rotated against the road, the roof bracing pattern is wrong, the cable-net mesh is a square bar grid, end-panel diagonals cut through each other, and the left_side camera is half occluded.

Counts: P0 = 0, P1 = 6, P2 = 8

## 2. Findings

| # | severity | view(s) | symptom (what the pixels show) | likely cause | finite fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | P1 | top, front_corner, aerial, rear, rear_north (compare sources/oblique.jpg, sources/top.jpg) | At the bend the two arms stop in separate end portals. A five/six-sided "gazebo" frame sits between them, with its own vertical posts, low top ring, no roof bracing and a flat dark-brown deck. In top.png it shows as a large dark hexagon. In both sources the arms meet in one sharp mitred V: top chords and bottom chords run straight to a single apex node, the roof members radiate from that node, and the deck and mesh railing fold around a sharp inner corner. | The apex was authored as a separate hub assembly placed between two independent straight trusses, not as one mitred V truss. | Remove the hub assembly. Extend both arms to the bisector plane and mitre the top and bottom chords into one apex node. Add the radiating roof members seen in the oblique source (3–4 members from the apex node to the nearest panel points). Fill the V deck to a sharp inner corner and run the mesh railing continuously around it. |
| 2 | P1 | top (governs), aerial, rear | Plan geometry against the road is wrong. In top.png the V is nearly symmetric about the road normal: the road-crossing arm meets the road axis at about 50°, and the land arm splays about 45° away from the road. In sources/top.jpg the crossing arm is close to perpendicular to the road (about 70°). The other arm runs almost alongside the road edge (about 20–25° off the road axis) over the sidewalk and building frontage. The included angle (about 90–95°) is right, but the whole V is rotated about 20–25° about the apex. | The apex angle was taken from the source but the V's orientation was set symmetric, not measured against the road axis. | Rotate the V about the apex by about 20–25° so the crossing arm is about 70° to the road axis and the land arm runs about 20–25° off the road along the left kerb and building frontage. Move the two end landings to match. Re-render top and check both angles against sources/top.jpg. |
| 3 | P1 | top, aerial, deck_walk, top_chord, truss_node, facade_close | Every bay of the roof (top lateral) bracing has a full X of crossing diagonals plus a transverse strut. In sources/top.jpg and sources/oblique.jpg each bay has one transverse strut and one diagonal, alternating direction into a zig-zag (Warren) pattern in plan. The X pattern doubles the member density and changes the dominant figure of the governing top view. | A generic X-brace module was used for the roof plane. | Replace the X pair in each roof bay with one diagonal, alternating direction bay by bay so the diagonals form a continuous zig-zag between the transverse struts. Re-render top and deck_walk. |
| 4 | P1 | glass_close, mesh_infill, truss_node, landing_stair, front, deck_walk (compare sources/front.png) | The infill between deck edge and handrail is a rigid orthogonal grid of thick bars: about 12 cm vertical spacing, horizontals every 20–25 cm. Keep the dark top rail. Add the thin vertical stainless posts at panel points (see finding 12). |

A Warren truss has no member crossings within one truss plane. | The last panel of the arm and the end portal frame each add their own diagonal, so they overlap. Or the panel count does not divide the arm length, and a partial panel was filled with both diagonals. | Recalculate the panel length so the arm divides into whole Warren panels ending on a node at the portal. Delete the duplicate diagonal. Make the end portal post the shared closing member, with no extra diagonal in the side plane. |

| 8 | P2 | deck_walk, glass_close, mesh_infill, top_chord, facade_close, road_under | The deck reads as brown planks with light grey joint lines, and the soffit is a plain solid slab. ## 4. Camera validity

- **left_side: not valid as a whole-envelope view (P1, finding 6).** The foreground context brick block hides about 40–45% of the bridge (the apex and the far arm).
- **front: valid but mis-matched.** Both arm ends are cut off by the frame edges, and the view is a flat elevation, not the source's deck-level perspective (P2, finding 10).
- **rear_side: valid.** The foreground roof block hides the apex in part, but both arms and both landings stay legible (polish only).
- **front_corner, aerial, top, right_side, rear: valid.** The whole envelope is legible, with nothing clipped.
- **Detail views:** facade_close, architecture_close, glass_close, truss_node, mesh_infill, deck_walk, landing_stair, road_under, top_chord and rear_north are all valid. Each frames its subject legibly, with no camera inside geometry.
