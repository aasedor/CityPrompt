# Planning in the Canadian city — teaching classification

The user teaches an undergraduate Canadian urban-planning course and requests
deep historical/recent research and classification of existing archetypes,
including eras such as the postwar 1950s. The class is international in outlook.
Earlier approved vocabulary separates use/form, aesthetic and planning approach.

## Chosen approach

Extend the existing exact-variant teaching overlay with an independently
reviewable history layer. Broad overlapping eras support browsing; short date
ranges and Canadian/international connections explain individual designs.
This retains contemporary infill and Vancouverism alongside older styles.
One exclusive style/era tree would lose revivals and cross-era housing forms;
an isolated essay would not categorise the catalogue students actually browse.
The multi-axis approach directly implements the user's requested distinctions.

## Scope and evidence

- Individually author history records for the 196 preserved current choices:
  97 buildings, 53 streets/pathways and 46 parks/open spaces. Preserve all eight
  reference conflicts as held, with no advertised era/style/approach assertion.
- Include the five exact-byte, independently reviewed original building
  candidates in the research export only; never activate them.
- Supply a 521-family coverage index: 497 legacy source-library families plus
  24 current-only native families identified by exact ID reconciliation. Tags inherited from exact
  reviewed choices describe those variants only; remaining source-only families
  are explicitly awaiting variant review, with no fabricated dates.
- Use primary Canadian municipal/federal/Indigenous/professional sources and
  international movement sources. Record access date, scope, historical/recent
  status and which facts they support. Distinguish source facts from our
  catalogue interpretations. Current policy must be checked as of 2026-10-25.
- Approximate design-era associations are not construction dates or claims of
  a real project's historical authorship. New originals may reference older
  movements. Cross-era and insufficient evidence are legitimate outcomes.
- Colonial surveying, Indigenous sovereignty and continuing planning, urban
  renewal/displacement, community resistance, social/co-operative housing,
  climate and equity belong in the research. Geometry cannot establish tenure,
  affordability, community authorship or Indigenous authority.
- Preserve existing locked teaching inputs and base overlay byte-for-byte.
  New authored inputs receive their own hash lock. Reject altered contracts,
  duplicate IDs, incomplete coverage and broken source/era references.

## Student experience

Add a visible Era browsing mode/filter to the local catalogue. Expand the
planning vocabulary to include Vancouverism, missing middle, compact infill,
modernist planning, heritage/reuse, public-life and mobility connections.
Keep introductory definitions short, with research detail in expandable sources
and the linked research report. Contemporary gabled and contextual brick are
plain descriptive aesthetics, not newly invented historical movements.
Filters combine across axes; reset and domain changes clear all active filters.
Keyboard and phone-width use must work. Exact identity/revision/method mismatch
withholds historical claims. No asset, model, placement, render, seed or backend
behaviour changes. No paid generation, activation, push or deployment.

## Deliverables and verification

Research synthesis/timeline and bibliography, 201-row current/candidate CSV,
521-row source coverage CSV, reproducible history JSON and local teaching UI.
Use finite authored mappings, not keyword-generated historical verdicts.
Run narrow generator/history/facet/card/palette tests and frontend type-check;
perform a read-only local browser trial and fresh whole-branch source review.
Retain the source branch locally and clearly separate research interpretation,
offline candidate approval, local UI testing and publication status.
