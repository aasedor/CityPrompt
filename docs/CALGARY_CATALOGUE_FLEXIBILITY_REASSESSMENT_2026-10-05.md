# Calgary catalogue reassessment with height proposals and relaxations

The current catalogue can support more student proposals than a strict current-district height comparison suggests. Of the **34 current placeable building variants**, **31 pass the existing preliminary use-and-height screen in at least one standard district**. The two churches have a listed discretionary worship use in S-CI but require the site's height rules; the passenger station requires an operator, authority and site review. None of these three should automatically be labelled an approved DC building.

The earlier suggestion to create 27 additional archetypes should be treated as a provisional variety list, not a minimum required to make the existing catalogue useful. Supplying hypothetical height modifiers increases the number of district types with at least three screened building options from **25 to 34 of the 68 district records**, before counting any relaxation proposals. This does not establish three approved options on every parcel.

Assessment date: October 5, 2026. The scope is the 34 current canonical building revisions, with the teaching programs recorded in `buildingPrograms.json`. Historical families and unapproved expansion assets are excluded. Heights below are native model envelopes, not surveyed Calgary bylaw heights. Building geometry, catalogue classifications and application behaviour have not been changed by this reassessment.

## How students can use the different approval routes

| Route | What the student proposes | How the catalogue should describe it |
|---|---|---|
| Standard district candidate | A listed use with a model that passes the preliminary height comparison | Keep the district's permitted or discretionary use category. State remaining parcel checks. |
| Height measurement review | Verify grade, roof measurement and any applicable exceptions before assuming a conflict | Show the model envelope and the applicable rule separately. |
| Relaxation assessment | A departure from an eligible development rule while retaining a listed use | Show the exact departure and the need for a planning decision. Do not imply an automatic tolerance or approval. |
| Land-use amendment | Change the mapped height modifier, density/FAR or district to support the proposal | Show existing and proposed designations separately. Only use modifiers the proposed district supports. |
| Direct Control proposal | Tailored regulation that an ordinary district cannot adequately provide | Require a reason that standard districts, including available relaxations, cannot achieve the proposal. |
| Site or program review | Resolve missing facts, such as station authority or a park café's principal food use | Keep the unresolved fact visible. A DC label does not resolve missing information. |

Calgary permits consideration of relaxations for permitted and discretionary uses, subject to the tests in [sections 30–31](https://www.calgary.ca/planning/land-use/online-land-use-bylaw.html?part=2&div=4) and [section 36](https://www.calgary.ca/planning/land-use/online-land-use-bylaw.html?part=2&div=5). The use must remain one prescribed for the land, and impacts on neighbouring properties and neighbourhood amenities matter. There is no general automatic percentage allowance in those tests.

[Section 40](https://www.calgary.ca/planning/land-use/online-land-use-bylaw.html?part=2&div=6) limits this flexibility. An unlisted use cannot be made acceptable by a height relaxation. Exceeding a mapped height normally calls for a land-use amendment, with specific exceptions for grade variations and certain elements above the eaveline without usable floor area. Contextual Single Detached Dwelling, Contextual Semi-detached Dwelling and Multi-Residential Development — Minor must comply with all applicable rules. An ordinary Single Detached Dwelling route must be distinguished from those contextual use forms.

[Section 20](https://www.calgary.ca/planning/land-use/online-land-use-bylaw.html?part=2&div=2) reserves DC for circumstances requiring regulation unavailable through other districts. It expressly rules out substituting DC where another district could achieve the same result with or without relaxations. This refines the classroom shorthand of putting anything unmatched into DC: students can explore DC, but should first identify why a standard district is insufficient.

M-H1 and M-H2 have base maximum heights with modifiers that lower those limits; they must not be treated as unlimited height-amendment containers.

## Revised priorities for additional archetypes

The strongest remaining needs concern uses and building forms. Height flexibility alone cannot create these programs or physical arrangements.

| Priority | Reason to add models |
|---|---|
| Manufactured homes | No current building is classified as Manufactured Home. A different residential style does not establish that use. |
| Workshops, small factories and warehouses | The catalogue lacks a useful range of ordinary industrial workplaces. The brewery and office tower do not provide that range. |
| Outdoor industrial yards and heavy industrial facilities | Different operations and site layouts are needed, with district-specific use checks. |
| Compact offices | The office tower is a poor physical substitute for everyday small offices, and the mixed office/retail tower does not solve pure-office use gaps. |
| Elementary schools and public infrastructure | The art school is post-secondary; works depots, maintenance buildings and utility facilities need their own programs. |
| Grade-oriented housing variety | More units with demonstrated individual access to grade would improve M-G/H-GO exercises. Height changes cannot turn shared upper-floor stairs into that arrangement. |
| Naturalization landscapes | Existing ornamental parks do not establish a Natural Area conservation or naturalization program. This remains a separate park catalogue need. |

Additional detached houses and mixed-use mid-rises can now be lower-priority variety work. The exact number needed for three options per district remains dependent on whether “options” means current-district candidates or proposed amendments, and whether parks count separately. A proposal route should not inflate the count of presently conforming options.

## Implications for the student interface

Retain the permitted and discretionary lists for current screening. Add a separate proposal section that explains the model's use, model height, current rule and proposed change. A student should be able to keep a building in the design while recording a proposed amendment, a relaxation request or a justified DC proposal. That record must not turn the proposal into an approved City designation.

The proposal section should distinguish height-measurement review, eligible relaxation assessment, a supported mapped-height amendment, a change of district and bespoke DC. Missing use or site facts stay visible. For parks, assess shelters and other structures individually rather than comparing tree height with a building limit.

The assessment is documentation only. The existing matching UI has not yet gained these proposal categories.
